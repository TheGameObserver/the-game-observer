When the United States drew Paraguay in Group C of their home World Cup, the common assumption was a comfortable opening win — a confidence-building three points before the harder tests against Uruguay and Senegal. Football, predictably, had other ideas.

The **1–1 draw at the Rose Bowl** was a microcosm of both teams' central tensions: the United States possess athleticism and individual quality that their collective tactical cohesion has not yet fully harnessed; Paraguay have a tactical intelligence that consistently punches above their ranking, if not quite the finishing quality to capitalise on it.

Both conclusions, by the way, are concerns for the respective coaching staffs heading into the remaining group games.

## Paraguay's Structural Brilliance in the First Period

Daniel Garnero has quietly built one of the most tactically coherent South American squads of this cycle, and his pre-match preparation for this fixture was immediately evident.

Paraguay set up in a **5–3–2 defensive shape**, transitioning to a **3–5–2 in possession** — a structure that mirrored, then challenged, the USA's own 4–3–3. The two wing-backs, **Gustavo Gómez** on the left and **Mathías Martínez** on the right, were the key figures in disrupting the American press.

When the USA pressed high in their favoured **4–3–3 press shape**, Paraguay's two centre-forwards dropped into midfield positions, creating a numerical overload in the zones the USA midfield trio were trying to cover. The result: the American midfielders were caught in perpetual decisions — press the ball or protect the zone?

> "Paraguay's solution to the American press was elegant. By dropping their forwards into the zones between the USA's midfield and forward lines, they created a constant relay pass option that broke the press at source."

This positional intelligence allowed Paraguay to build patiently through the thirds for long stretches of the first half, even as the USA's superior athleticism created counter-pressing moments.

## The USA Goal: Power Over Subtlety

The United States took the lead on **34 minutes** through a goal that owed nothing to tactical sophistication and everything to the blunt force of modern football athleticism.

A long diagonal from **Tyler Adams** found **Folarin Balogun** in the right half-space. Balogun, operating as an underlapping right forward, held his marker — Rodriguez — with his back to goal, turned sharply, and drove 25 yards before releasing a low shot that deflected off Fabrício Bustamante and looped over the goalkeeper.

Not how Berhalter would have drawn it up on the tactics board. Entirely in keeping with how the United States have historically scored their most important goals — through improvisation, athleticism, and the will to create something from apparent nothing.

**1–0, Rose Bowl delirious.**

## Paraguay's Adjustment: How the Albirroja Found Their Goal

The equaliser came on **71 minutes**, and it was the product of two critical decisions by Garnero at half-time.

First, he shifted **Carlos González** higher, asking him to pin the USA's holding midfielder Adams more aggressively. Second, he moved to a **3–4–3 in possession** in the second half, pushing both wing-backs into attacking positions simultaneously — an attacking overload that the USA's narrower 4–3–3 struggled to handle without exposing space between the full-back and the centre-back.

**Miguel Almirón** — now 32 but playing with the energy of someone chasing a last World Cup moment — was the central figure.

He received in space on the left inside channel, drifted past two USA press triggers, and cut across the penalty area. **Ramón Sosa's run** beyond the far post created a decoy that pulled the last USA centre-back into an impossible position. The cut-back was perfectly weighted. **Sosa swept it home first time.**

**1–1. The Rose Bowl fell silent.**

## The Final Quarter: Chaos, Chances, and Nothing Settled

The last 20 minutes were the best advertisement for the randomness that makes football endlessly watchable.

The USA created three clear chances — a Tyler Adams long-range effort that clipped the crossbar, a **Christian Pulisic** penalty claim controversially waved away, and a **Josh Sargent** header from six yards that somehow cleared the bar. Paraguay hit the post with a López free-kick in added time.

On another night, six different results were available. Football chose the one that served neither team perfectly.

## Tactical Verdict: What Did We Learn?

**USA's attacking structure** remains their primary tactical limitation. Berhalter's 4–3–3 is set up to press aggressively and transition at pace, but the central overload that Paraguay's 5–3–2 created showed that against organised opposition with positional intelligence, the USA lack a genuine ball progression mechanism through midfield.

Adams is excellent as a destroyer. He is less effective as a build-up organiser, and the USA have built a system that demands he perform both roles simultaneously.

**Pulisic's positioning** is a perpetual source of tension. He is their most creative player but insists on operating in the left halfspace where the density of players limits his impact. A more central role — perhaps a return to the 10 position behind two forwards — seems the obvious solution, and the obvious question is why Berhalter has resisted it.

For **Paraguay**, the conclusions are more encouraging. They exposed a real defensive fragility in the USA's high defensive line. Almirón's movement was a constant problem. If Sosa and López can find their finishing touch against Senegal and Uruguay, the Albirroja are very much capable of progressing.

## Player Ratings

**Tyler Adams (USA) — 7.0.** Outstanding in the defensive phases, sharp in the press, and the assist for the opener was world-class. The ball retention numbers were occasionally worrying — 83% against a team inviting the USA to play — but his engine and positional discipline remain the USA's most important individual qualities.

**Christian Pulisic (USA) — 6.5.** Frustrated by Paraguay's narrow defensive shape and his own positional rigidity. His quality is beyond question. The system's relationship with that quality remains the persistent issue.

**Miguel Almirón (Paraguay) — 8.5.** The best player on the pitch. His second-half involvement was incessant, creative, and ultimately decisive. Still elite.

**Ramón Sosa (Paraguay) — 8.0.** The goal was exceptional in its composure. A player the Premier League clubs watching in the stands will have noted carefully.

## The Group Picture

The USA sit **third in Group C** on one point after this result, with Uruguay defeating Senegal 2–1 in the day's other fixture. The math is already tight. A win against Senegal on Matchday 2 becomes imperative.

Paraguay, meanwhile, face Uruguay next — a genuine test of whether Garnero's structure can contain South American opposition of a different order. Given tonight's performance, the answer might surprise a few people.

The home World Cup was always going to demand something extraordinary from the United States. One point from the opener suggests the extraordianry will be needed sooner than anyone hoped.
