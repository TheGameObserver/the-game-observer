There are 87,000 ways to interpret a football match, and inside the Estadio Azteca last night, all 87,000 of them were wearing green.

For Mexico, the emotion of this opening game — their **first World Cup on home soil since 1986** — was always going to be something beyond football. But football, eventually and emphatically, delivered. **Hirving Lozano's two goals**, combined with a second-half team performance of genuine quality, produced the 2–0 win that the occasion demanded and the group table required.

For **Bafana Bafana**, there were encouraging passages — particularly in the first half-hour — but the gulf between aspiration and execution proved too wide to bridge against a Mexican team playing with the combined pressure and freedom that only a home World Cup can produce.

## The Pre-Match Atmosphere That Changed the Game

It would be journalistically irresponsible not to acknowledge the context. The Azteca was vibrating before kick-off in a way that has been described frequently but must be witnessed to be properly understood. The noise during the Mexican national anthem was, by any measurable standard, the loudest this correspondent has heard at a football match.

And that noise had a tactical consequence.

South Africa's head coach **Hugo Broos** had designed his team to press high in the first 15 minutes — to impose uncertainty early on Mexico and force the home side's technically capable but mentally fragile midfielders into rushed decision-making.

The plan dissolved inside three minutes. **The crowd made Mexico brave.** Every Mexican touch drew a wall of sound; every South African ball-win was met with immediate hostility. Broos' press, which requires collective confidence and clear triggering moments, never found its rhythm in that environment.

## Mexico's Positional Control — The Blueprint for Group Progress

**Javier Aguirre** has been criticised throughout the qualification cycle for overly conservative selection and an excessive reliance on the 4–4–2 defensive compact. This performance, tonight, suggested he has been waiting for the right moment to reveal something more expansive.

Mexico played in a **4–3–3 with high width**, using **Lozano** on the right and **Alexis Vega** on the left as genuine wide attackers who inverted to create central overloads. The mechanism was elegant: when Lozano cut inside onto his left foot, right-back **Jorge Sánchez** pushed into the right channel to provide the overlapping threat. When South Africa's left-back tracked Sánchez, the central space became available for **Edson Álvarez** to drive forward from his holding position.

Three passes, three positions changed, the entire defensive structure of South Africa manipulated. It was the most sophisticated tactical display El Tri have produced since the 2018 win over Germany.

## The Goals: Precision and Personality

**Goal 1, 28 minutes.** Lozano received the ball from Álvarez 30 yards from goal, adjusted his feet with two sharp touches, and struck a rising shot into the far top corner that gave goalkeeper Ronwen Williams absolutely no chance.

The celebration — arms spread wide, face to the sky, absorbing the wall of noise — said everything about what this moment meant. **Lozano had announced in the pre-tournament press conference that this would be his final World Cup**, at 31, and he is playing with that specific kind of liberation that farewell performances sometimes unlock.

> "When a player knows it's their last time, they stop playing to avoid making mistakes. They start playing to make moments. Lozano tonight was playing to make moments."

**Goal 2, 61 minutes.** If the first was power, the second was craft. A training-ground corner routine — unexpected from an Aguirre side — released **Lozano at the back post** with a glancing header that looped into the net. Deceptive pace, perfect trajectory, goalkeeper stranded.

**2–0, and the Azteca became a cathedral of noise.**

## South Africa's Problems: The Promising and the Concerning

The scoreline invites a dismissal of Bafana Bafana, and that would be unjust.

In the first 25 minutes, before Lozano's opener fundamentally altered the game's emotional temperature, South Africa played with genuine conviction. **Percy Tau** — operating as a false nine — dropped deep to receive between Mexico's midfield line and created consistent half-turn moments. **Themba Zwane** on the right was quick and direct, and twice exposed the gap between **Montes** and **Sánchez** in Mexico's backline.

The problem was the final ball. Twice in promising positions — at 0–0, when the match was still genuinely open — South Africa's attackers chose complex dribbling routes when simpler passes to runners were available. The decision-making was nervous, reflecting a team aware of the moment's weight and not yet freed from it.

After the first goal, Bafana's structure opened up. Their 4–3–3 press became a 4–5–1 deep block, conceding the midfield territory they had contested, and Mexico — suddenly free to circulate — became genuinely difficult to stop.

## Tactical Notes: What Mexico Must Improve Before Uruguay

This was an encouraging performance, not a perfect one.

**Mexico's defensive transition** remains a concern. On four separate occasions in the second half, South Africa broke at pace and found Mexico's back four exposed. The saves were routine — Guillermo Ochoa's composure is the constant — but against **Uruguay's Luis Suárez-led attack**, the same spaces will be exploited with higher quality execution.

**Álvarez's positioning** in the second half drifted too high, leaving the holding midfielder role effectively vacant. Aguirre noticed, and the introduction of **Rodolfo Pizarro** in the 74th minute to sit deeper was the correct correction, but the problem is one of player habit rather than tactical instruction.

**Vega was excellent** for 55 minutes and then faded badly, suggesting his fitness for a full 90 minutes at this level is still not complete after his recent knee issue. Aguirre will need to manage his minutes carefully.

## Player Ratings

**Guillermo Ochoa — 7.5.** Four good saves, commanding in the air, and continues to defy time and logic in roughly equal measure. Still the best goalkeeper Mexico has produced in a generation.

**Edson Álvarez — 8.0.** The heartbeat of Mexico's first-half control. Twenty-seven successful passes in the opponent's half, three key defensive interventions, and the assist for goal one. Becoming a genuinely complete modern midfielder.

**Hirving Lozano — 9.5.** Two goals, one assist, seven dribbles completed, and the emotional leader of a team that needed someone to wear the occasion as a crown rather than a burden. His final World Cup has begun exactly as it should.

**Percy Tau (South Africa) — 7.0.** The best player in green. His movement was clever and his reading of spaces often superior to his teammates. A talent this World Cup deserved to see on a bigger stage.

## Looking Ahead

Mexico face **Poland on Matchday 2** — a genuine test that will do far more to define their Group B trajectory than this comfortable opener. A win would put them in complete control of their progression; a loss would force them into a do-or-die final game against a team who have surprised everyone.

South Africa must now beat Poland and hope. Their squad has the character for a positive result. Whether they have the execution is the remaining question.

Last night, though, belonged to Mexico, to Lozano, and to 87,000 voices that turned a football stadium into something between a church and a wave.
